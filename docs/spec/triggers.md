# Triggers: what happens by itself

## Rules

- **Every outward step has a trigger.** A request, chase, update, order or pack goes when its moment arrives (an event), not when someone remembers. Where: `service.ts automaticSteps`, `FIRST_REQUESTS`, `CLIENT_UPDATE_TEMPLATES`, the machine's derive loop (`contract_pack_requested`, `signed_transfer_requested`, `exchange_conditions_met`).
- **Each thing goes when it is ready, once**: the mortgage deed when the offer clears, the contract when approved, the TR1 request on exchange. Not bundled behind an unrelated step.
- **Missed moments are caught up**: the daily tick re-checks anything ready but not sent (e.g. deeds ready to sign). A case enrolled late, or moved on by hand, still gets its sends.
- **Trust level decides send vs propose**, per action and subject (`engine_action_level`): `auto` sends, `propose` puts it on the Tasks list to approve, `assist` acts and asks for a confirming look (the engine's auto-clears). The trigger is the same either way.
- **"No" to a message is not "no" to the step.** A person rejecting an essential send (ID check, proof of funds, signing pack, search order, deposit/balance request, property forms, exchange authority, ownership, insurance) leaves a **Send It** task while the case still needs it (`due.ts resend:`). System withdrawals (duplicates, already arrived) do not.
- **Every send is checked** (`messageProblem`): a broken message becomes a task, never an email. A failed send becomes a **Not Sent** task with Try Again.
- **Incoming email is never filed away unseen.** Its words are read as a note (`files.ts fileEmailBodyAsDocument`), even when the document reader half-recognised it. What it says becomes a proposal (an issue, a request, a client decision); when the reader is not sure of anything, and the sender is outside the firm, it is a **Read And Reply** task (`<Who> Email` chip). Low confidence is a reason to ask a person, never to do nothing. The only email that may need no reply is a **pure acknowledgement** ("Will do, cheers", "Thanks, received"), and only when two checks agree: the codified rule (`notes.ts isAcknowledgement`: thanks/acknowledgement words only; no question, number, attachment, "but"/"not", never an answer word like "ok", "yes", "fine") and then the AI reading it against the conversation and what the case is waiting for (`ai.ts ClaudeAckChecker`; no checker or a failed one = not an acknowledgement). Even then it goes by the `email_no_reply` trust level per sender: at **Propose** (default) it is a one-click task, "Confirm no reply is needed to the client's acknowledgement: “…”"; only at **Auto** is it filed without one. Any email task can be approved as dealt with, so a person can always let an email go.
- **A client's email is answered from the case, point by point.** The reader splits it into lines (a question, "that's done", "that's waiting", "send it again", a problem, a decision, dates); a question is never "information only". One reply answering every point is drafted from the case facts (`reply.ts replyFacts`: what is done, waiting and on whom, what is next, and timing such as the lender's certificate-of-title notice), worded by the drafter or assembled from the facts. The task is named by what the email was read as (`notes.ts replyTitle`), the way an issue's chip names it: "Reply to the client's request for a status update", "…request for their report on title", a reported problem as the problem itself ("Seller threatening to pull out"), "…update on their ID", "Answer the client's question: “…”", else "Reply to the client's email": tick the lines, edit the reply, approve, and both go. "Done" is noted on the matching wait and pauses its chase (`record_client_progress`), never clears it; "send it again" re-sends that request with its links (Chase Now). A document they ask for ("can you send over the searches") is found on the file and **attached to the reply** (`NoteMessage.attach`): one email, nothing sent separately. The report on title is the one the client was sent (the approved draft, as the Word document), never a title document; a specific name ("report on title") is never matched by a general word in it ("title"). Not on the file (or not sent yet) = nothing attached, the reply says we will send it as soon as we can, and a task to find it. The reply does not raise an issue the client has already been emailed about (`IssueState.clientToldAt`: an Update The Client step, or the reply to the email that raised it; cleared when the issue gets worse) as news; a status question still gets one line that it is open. A status update ("what's going on?") is at most five sentences, the most serious open problem first. What the client told us that is not yet recorded (a task still pending: "my lender has rescinded my offer") is in the reply's facts and is never contradicted. A problem read from an email never holds less than its kind (a reported risk to the deal holds exchange, whatever the reader said).
- **Updates to the other side** (`counterparty-status.ts`). What the other side's solicitor and the estate agent may be told is decided in one place.
  - **Shared:** the progress of the work (searches, enquiries, the contract pack, replies, management pack, redemption statement, signed contract held); that the mortgage offer is in or awaited (status only, never its terms); whether we are ready to exchange, or what is outstanding and whether it is with them; the target dates.
  - **Never shared:** the client's finances, gifts, source of funds, ID or AML results, the content of any problem or negotiation, or the client's reasons or circumstances.
  - **The client's own chain (their sale or purchase):** only once the client has said we may (`record_chain_consent`, from their email or a person). Until then, a question about it is answered "we will take our client's instructions".
  - **Their "any update?" email:** the reply to them (and every message to them, including issue steps) is drafted only from these facts, never from the client's brief.
  - **Milestones they hear without asking:** each goes once per recipient, with trust level `counterparty_update` (default Propose, so each is a task to approve, shown as *Other Side Update* or *Agent Update*). Acting for the buyer: *our searches are back* (to their solicitor) and *our client's mortgage offer is in* (to their solicitor and the agent). Either side: *we are ready to exchange* (to their solicitor and the agent). Migration 117.
- **Files go to a client as a secure link, not an attachment** (`lib/server/file-shares.ts`, migration 116). Any file sent to a client goes as a link in the email: a copy they asked for, the report on title, a completion statement. The link opens with a six-digit code emailed to the client's address on the case. Codes last 15 minutes with five tries; a link lasts 60 days; a verified browser stays open for a day. Opens and downloads are counted. The page (`/f/<token>`) is written for anyone: one step at a time, no account. If a link cannot be made, the files go attached rather than not at all.
  - **Attachments instead, only when it comes up.** There is no setting up front.
    - On a reply with files, the task offers **Secure Link / Attachments**, and with Attachments, **Always For This Client**.
    - A client who writes "can you just attach it", "the link won't open" or "I never got the code" gets a line: *Client asks for files as attachments instead of a secure link*. Approving it sends the last link's files attached with the reply, and files go to them as attachments from then on (`set_file_delivery`, `MatterState.fileDelivery`). Only the client (or a colleague) can make that choice.
- **An email's task is a checklist of sub-tasks**: case changes (raise an issue, mark the mortgage offer withdrawn, note "done", re-send a form, record a decision or dates) and messages. Who is written to is a rule table (`recipients.ts RECIPIENT_RULES`): the client always gets a reply (anyone else when they asked something); the client reporting the deal at risk also means the other side's solicitor (ticked) and the agent (offered); a delay or chain problem means the other side; dates the client wants are offered to the other side; a withdrawn offer offers the lender. Each message is drafted from the case (to the other side: formal, nothing confidential), editable, ticked or not; Approve does every ticked part. An issue raised from such a task does not also fire its own automatic follow-up message. New situations are new rows in the table, not new code paths.
- **Sandboxes never reach a real mailbox** (`sandbox.ts routeEach`): every sender method is routed to the outbox; a real file uploaded to a sandbox is still read.

## Adding one

A new trigger names: the event, the recipient, the template (in Email Templates), the trust-level key, and what happens if it is rejected (does the case still need it → add to the resend set). If it opens a wait, see [waits.md](waits.md).
